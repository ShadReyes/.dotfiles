# Etsy tag research

Public guidance checked 2026-10-22. These sources support the selection method. The separate [Marketplace Insights procedure](marketplace-insights.md) explains the dated first-party research behind the revised base and how to refresh it. Recheck platform rules during use. Summaries below are paraphrases.

## Platform rules and recommendations

- [Etsy Help: How to Use Tags to Get Found in Search](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336307-How-to-Use-Tags-to-Get-Found-in-Search). Maximum 13 tags, each at most 20 characters. Permitted characters are letters, numbers, spaces, and specified accented letters; apostrophes and hyphens may occur internally, not at the start. Consult the page's character list for non-ASCII tags. These are platform limits; using all slots is a recommendation.
- [Etsy Seller Handbook: Keywords 101](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/382774281517), updated August 26, 2025. Etsy recommends all 13 slots, descriptive multi-word phrases, useful synonyms, and specific buyer intent. Categories and attributes already contribute search terms, so exact duplicate tags add no coverage; longer descriptive phrases can still help. Avoid duplicate tags, intentional misspellings, plural-only variants, and mixing languages. Use Shop Stats to guide changes. For a query longer than 20 characters, use multiple meaningful phrases. Keep titles readable and descriptions natural. Search considers more than tags, so these practices do not guarantee rank or conversion.
- [Etsy Help: Marketplace Insights](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/35122361353239-How-Do-I-Use-Etsy-s-Marketplace-Insights-Tool). Describes Etsy search counts, competing listings, and related terms. The authenticated interface observed on October 22 also exposed relative conversion labels. Retain the actual live period, labels, and definitions; availability and features can change. Search counts are not purchases, and conversion labels are not exact percentages or listing-specific performance.

## Additional research methods

- [eRank: Use eRank to Find Tags for Your Etsy Listings](https://help.erank.com/blog/use-erank-to-find-tags-for-your-etsy-listings/), April 29, 2025. Use keyword ideas and market-specific search/click/competition data to compare relevant phrases. Its article describes average monthly metrics over a 12-month window and estimated competition; preserve the live tool's actual labels and period. One-word tags often offer little specificity. Competitor tags provide candidates, not proof those tags caused sales.
- [Marmalead: How to Use Its Search Tool](https://blog.marmalead.com/how-to-use-marmaleads-search-tool-an-etsy-seo-tutorial/), November 1, 2023. Start from a product term, explore narrower buyer phrases, and compare searches, engagement, competition, and seasonality. Related searches can broaden candidates. Verify live metric definitions; article examples are not current data for another product.
- [eRank: Your Etsy Tag Questions Answered](https://help.erank.com/blog/your-etsy-tag-questions-answered/), March 5, 2025. Recommends retaining priority phrases in both title and tags while diversifying the set.
- [Marmalead: Best Tags for Etsy](https://blog.marmalead.com/best-tags-for-etsy/), December 1, 2025, recommends mixing specific and broader phrases grounded in buyer intent. Its long example queries can exceed Etsy's tag limit; validate any proposed tag independently.
- [Marmalead: Etsy Listing Grades](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-listing-grades/), August 7, 2024, describes tool scores favoring unique words across titles and tags. That conflicts with eRank's priority-phrase repetition advice. These are vendor recommendations, not platform requirements.

## Resolve conflicting advice

Use official Etsy guidance when tool advice conflicts with it. A tool's title/tag-match score does not require copying all tags into a title. High search counts cannot justify irrelevant tags, and low competition can reflect low demand. Repeating a word in useful phrases is different from spending slots on near-identical tags. A phrase can be descriptive without having verified long-tail demand; label its evidence accurately.

The four-base/nine-dynamic allocation and candidate examples are local working choices informed by the dated research. Change the allocation when relevance or observed buyer behavior supports it. No source establishes this split as optimal.
